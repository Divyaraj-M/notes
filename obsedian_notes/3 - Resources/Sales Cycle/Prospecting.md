# 02 · Prospecting & Demand Generation

> [!abstract] Goal
> Create enough qualified conversations to fill the top of the funnel — through outbound, inbound and partners.

|  |  |
|---|---|
| **Phase** | Before a deal exists |
| **Owner** | SDR / BDR + Marketing + Partnerships |

## Process — step by step

### 1. Build the working list
Pull contacts matching ICP from the data provider. Verify emails, enrich with role, tenure and trigger signals.

- **Metrics to measure:** list size vs target · email bounce rate (keep <2%) · data accuracy % on spot-check · % of records fully enriched

### 2. Segment by trigger
Group by *why now* — funding round, new exec hire, hiring for a relevant role, competitor renewal window, product signal, event attendance.

- **Metrics to measure:** number of live trigger segments · reply rate per trigger type · % of total outreach that is trigger-based (vs generic)

### 3. Write sequenced messaging
Per segment: 8–12 touches over 3–4 weeks across email, phone, LinkedIn and video. Lead with the problem, not the product. One ask per touch.

- **Metrics to measure:** open rate · reply rate · **positive**-reply rate · meeting-booked rate per sequence · touches required per meeting · unsubscribe/spam rate

### 4. Run inbound capture in parallel
Content, SEO, webinars, review sites, paid. Every asset routes to a form; every form routes to a scored lead in the CRM.

- **Metrics to measure:** MQLs per channel · form conversion rate · cost per MQL · content-to-MQL rate · organic vs paid mix

### 5. Work the inbound within minutes
Speed-to-lead is the single biggest lever on inbound conversion. Route hot leads to a live rep, not a queue.

- **Metrics to measure:** median first-response time (target <5 min) · % contacted inside SLA · conversion rate banded by response time

### 6. Work partner and referral sources
Existing customers, integration partners, resellers, and champions who changed jobs — historically the highest-converting source you have.

- **Metrics to measure:** % of pipeline from referral/partner · referral win rate vs outbound win rate · partner-sourced ARR · number of tracked champion job-moves

### 7. Log and iterate weekly
Track reply rate, positive-reply rate and meeting-booked rate per sequence. Kill anything under benchmark and rewrite it.

- **Metrics to measure:** activities per rep per day · dials-to-conversation ratio · meetings **booked vs held** per rep per week · no-show rate · sequences retired per month

## Stage scorecard

| Metric | What it tells you | How to calculate | Directional benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meetings held per rep per week | The real output of the stage | Held meetings ÷ reps ÷ weeks | 8–12 mid-market SDR |
| Positive-reply rate | Message-market fit | Positive replies ÷ contacts sequenced | 2–5% cold outbound |
| Speed-to-lead | Inbound execution discipline | Median minutes, first touch | <5 min for hot inbound |
| Cost per opportunity | Whether the channel scales | Channel spend ÷ opps created | Compare against ACV |
| No-show rate | Booking quality | No-shows ÷ booked | <25%, reconfirm to reduce |

## What you need going in
- Target account list from [[ICP Planning]]
- Intent / trigger data
- Content and case studies
- Sequencing tool and dialler

## What this stage produces
- Booked and accepted discovery meetings
- Scored leads in the CRM
- Message-market fit learnings per segment

> [!success] Exit criteria
> A meeting is booked **and accepted** on the calendar with a named person who fits the ICP — not just a lead in a list.

## Where deals die here
- Spraying one generic sequence at every segment
- Chasing volume of leads instead of quality of accounts
- No follow-up on no-shows — usually a third of booked meetings
