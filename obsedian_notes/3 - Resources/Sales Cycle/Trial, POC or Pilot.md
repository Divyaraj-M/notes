# 06 · Trial, POC or Pilot

> [!abstract] Goal
> Let the buyer prove it themselves, against pre-agreed success criteria, in a bounded window.

|  |  |
|---|---|
| **Phase** | Working the deal |
| **Owner** | AE + Solutions Engineer + Customer Success (shadow) |
| **Typical duration** | 7–30 days — cap it |
| **Previous** | [[Demo & Solution Design]] |
| **Next** | [[Business Case & Proposal]] |

## Process — step by step

### 1. Write success criteria first
3–5 measurable outcomes agreed **in writing** before access is granted. "We'll evaluate it" is not a criterion.

- **Metrics to measure:** % of trials with written criteria · number of criteria per trial · conversion rate with vs without written criteria

### 2. Scope narrowly
One team, one workflow, one use case. Broad pilots stall; narrow ones produce a result.

- **Metrics to measure:** use cases per pilot · users in scope · scope-change events mid-pilot

### 3. Set the timeline and the decision date
Start date, mid-point check-in, end date, and the date they commit to deciding after it.

- **Metrics to measure:** % of trials with both an end date and a decision date · average trial length · extension rate (each extension drops close odds)

### 4. Configure the environment for them
Don't hand over an empty tenant. Pre-build the workflows, import their data, connect the key integration, set up users.

- **Metrics to measure:** time to configure (hours) · % pre-configured before handover · integration connection rate

### 5. Train the users
A short enablement session plus a one-page quick-start. Adoption in a trial is a leading indicator of adoption after purchase.

- **Metrics to measure:** % of trial users trained · training attendance rate · time from access to first login

### 6. Monitor usage actively
Watch logins, feature use, and drop-off. If usage stalls by day 3, intervene — don't wait for the end-of-trial call.

- **Metrics to measure:** DAU/WAU during trial · day-3 and day-7 login rate · activation rate (key action completed) · feature adoption breadth · % of trials flagged stalled

### 7. Run the mid-point review
What's working, what's blocked, are we tracking to the criteria. Adjust before it's too late to save.

- **Metrics to measure:** mid-point review completion rate · % of stalled trials recovered after intervention · blockers raised per trial

### 8. Close the pilot with a results readout
Present measured results against each criterion, side by side with the pain cost from [[Discovery]]. Get the champion to co-present internally.

- **Metrics to measure:** % of criteria met · readout attendance including economic buyer · trial → paid conversion · days from readout to proposal

## Stage scorecard

| Metric | What it tells you | How to calculate | Directional benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trial → paid conversion | Whether the pilot proves anything | Paid ÷ trials completed | 25–50% assisted trials |
| Activation rate in trial | Real product engagement, not logins | Users hitting key action ÷ users provisioned | >60% |
| Time-to-first-value | Speed of proof | Days from access to first meaningful outcome | <7 days |
| % of criteria met | Objective result vs opinion | Criteria met ÷ criteria set | >80% to advance |
| Extension rate | Decision-avoidance signal | Trials extended ÷ trials run | <20% |

## What you need going in
- Agreed, written success criteria
- Sandbox / trial tenant
- Sample or real customer data
- Enablement material

## What this stage produces
- Measured results per criterion
- Usage and adoption data
- Internal champion armed with proof
- Technical validation complete

> [!success] Exit criteria
> Success criteria are met and acknowledged **in writing**, and the buyer commits to a commercial conversation.

## Where deals die here
- Open-ended trials with no end date
- No success criteria, so the result is a matter of opinion
- Handing over an empty environment
- Letting a silent trial run to expiry
