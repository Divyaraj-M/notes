# 09 · Security, Legal & Procurement

> [!abstract] Goal
> Clear the institutional gates that quietly own most of your cycle length in mid-market and enterprise deals.

|  |  |
|---|---|
| **Phase** | Getting to signature |
| **Owner** | AE quarterbacks; Security/IT, Legal and Finance execute |
| **Typical duration** | 1–8 weeks — start it early |
| **Previous** | [[Negotiation & Objection Handling]] |
| **Next** | [[Close & Contract Execution]] |

> [!tip] The highest-leverage change in this entire cycle is starting this stage **in parallel** with negotiation rather than after it.

## Process — step by step

### 1. Trigger it in parallel, not at the end
Start security and legal review as soon as terms are broadly agreed.

- **Metrics to measure:** % of deals with security started before verbal agreement · average days saved on parallel vs sequential deals · overlap days between stages 8 and 9

### 2. Send the trust pack proactively
SOC 2 / ISO 27001 report, pen test summary, DPA, sub-processor list, architecture and data-flow overview, uptime history.

- **Metrics to measure:** % of deals receiving the pack before being asked · reduction in questionnaire volume when sent proactively · trust-pack open/read rate

### 3. Complete the security questionnaire
Route to the security team with a deadline. Reuse previous answers from a maintained response library.

- **Metrics to measure:** days in security review · questionnaires per quarter · % of answers reused from the library · first-pass approval rate

### 4. Handle privacy and data residency
DPA, GDPR / regional requirements, where data is stored and processed, retention and deletion policy, AI and data-usage terms.

- **Metrics to measure:** % of deals requiring a DPA · DPA turnaround days · % of deals with residency requirements · deals lost to residency or AI-terms gaps

### 5. Run legal redlines
Send your paper first if you can. Track redlines centrally; escalate only genuine deviations — liability cap, indemnity, termination, auto-renewal, SLA.

- **Metrics to measure:** redline cycles per contract · days in legal · % of deals on your own paper · most-negotiated clause frequency · legal hours per deal

### 6. Clear procurement and vendor onboarding
Vendor forms, tax documents, insurance certificates, supplier portal registration, PO issuance, banking details.

- **Metrics to measure:** days in procurement · time to PO issuance · % of deals requiring vendor-portal onboarding · % blocked on the buyer's fiscal calendar

### 7. Confirm accessibility and compliance extras where relevant
VPAT, HIPAA, industry-specific certifications.

- **Metrics to measure:** % of deals requiring compliance extras · win rate where required vs not · time cost per requirement

### 8. Track every open item on the close plan
One shared list, one owner per item, one date per item. Chase daily near the end.

- **Metrics to measure:** open items count per deal · overdue items · % of deals slipping past the forecast date from this stage

## Stage scorecard

| Metric | What it tells you | How to calculate | Directional benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days in security review | Where your cycle time actually goes | Median days entry → sign-off | <10 mid-market, <30 enterprise |
| Days in legal | Contracting friction | Median days first redline → agreed | <14 |
| Redline cycles per contract | Whether your standard paper is reasonable | Count of round trips | ≤2 |
| % of deals slipping here | The real forecast risk | Slipped deals ÷ deals in stage | <20% |
| First-pass security approval rate | Trust-pack quality | Approved without follow-up ÷ total | >60% |

## What you need going in
- Security documentation pack, current and dated
- MSA / DPA templates
- Questionnaire response library
- Insurance and tax documents

## What this stage produces
- Security approval
- Signed DPA
- Agreed redlines
- PO or vendor record created

> [!success] Exit criteria
> Security signs off, legal agrees the paper, and procurement has issued a PO or its equivalent.

## Where deals die here
- Starting security review after the forecast date
- Not knowing the buyer's procurement calendar or fiscal year end
- A champion who doesn't know how to navigate their own procurement
- Unmanaged redlines going round in circles
