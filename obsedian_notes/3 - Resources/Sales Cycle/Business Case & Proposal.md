# 07 · Business Case & Proposal

> [!abstract] Goal
> Give the champion a document that wins the internal argument when you're not in the room.

|  |  |
|---|---|
| **Phase** | Working the deal |
| **Owner** | AE, with Deal Desk and Finance on pricing |
| **Typical duration** | 2–7 days |
| **Previous** | [[Trial, POC or Pilot]] |
| **Next** | [[Negotiation & Objection Handling]] |

## Process — step by step

### 1. Quantify the return
Build the ROI model from **their** numbers, not yours: cost of current state, cost of solution, payback period, three-year value. Show the assumptions.

- **Metrics to measure:** modelled ROI % · payback period (months) · 3-year net value · % of proposals with an ROI model attached · win rate with vs without a model

### 2. Choose the commercial structure
Edition, seats, usage tier, term length, ramp, payment terms. Model 2–3 options — a base, a recommended, and an upside.

- **Metrics to measure:** options presented per proposal · % choosing the recommended (middle) option · average contract term · % multi-year · annual-prepay rate

### 3. Get pricing approved
Run non-standard discounts, terms or custom clauses through deal desk / finance **before** showing them to the buyer.

- **Metrics to measure:** deal desk turnaround time · % of deals requiring non-standard approval · approval exception rate · rework loops per quote

### 4. Write the proposal
Their problem in their words, the measured pilot results, the proposed solution, implementation scope, pricing options, terms, and a mutual action plan with dates.

- **Metrics to measure:** time to produce a proposal (hours) · template reuse rate · proposal version count before acceptance

### 5. Build the mutual close plan
Every remaining step to go-live — security review, legal, procurement, signature, kickoff — with owners and dates, agreed by both sides.

- **Metrics to measure:** % of opps with a mutual action plan (MAP) · MAP task completion % · forecast accuracy for MAP vs non-MAP deals · overdue MAP items per deal

### 6. Walk them through it live
Never email a proposal cold. Present it, watch reactions, take the objections live, then send the document.

- **Metrics to measure:** % presented live vs emailed · close rate live vs emailed · objections raised in the walkthrough

### 7. Confirm the approval path
Exactly who signs, in what order, and what each of them needs to see. Ask what has killed similar proposals internally before.

- **Metrics to measure:** approvers identified per deal · % with signatory confirmed by name · number of approvers discovered *after* pricing was locked (should be zero)

## Stage scorecard

| Metric | What it tells you | How to calculate | Directional benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proposal → close rate | Proposal quality and timing | Won ÷ proposals sent | 40–60% mid-market |
| Average discount | Price integrity | 1 − (net price ÷ list) | <15% healthy |
| Days proposal → close | Late-stage momentum | Median days | 14–30 mid-market |
| Deal size vs original scope | Whether scope shrank to close | Final ACV ÷ proposed ACV | >0.9 |
| % of proposals with MAP | Late-stage forecast reliability | MAP deals ÷ proposals | >80% |

## What you need going in
- Pilot results from [[Trial, POC or Pilot]]
- Cost-of-pain numbers from [[Discovery]]
- Price book and discount policy
- Legal templates

## What this stage produces
- ROI / business case
- Formal proposal or quote
- Mutual action plan with dates
- Named approval chain

> [!success] Exit criteria
> The proposal is delivered and verbally accepted in principle, with the approval path and dates agreed.

## Where deals die here
- ROI built on vendor-supplied averages instead of the buyer's numbers
- Emailing the proposal without presenting it
- Discovering a second approver after pricing is locked
- No dates on the close plan
