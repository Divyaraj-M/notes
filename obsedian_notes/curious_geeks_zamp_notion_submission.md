## 1. One-line pitch

**Curious Geeks is a pre-launch feedback platform where builders share what they are building, get useful feedback early, and improve before they launch.**

Product Hunt celebrates finished products.  
Curious Geeks helps products become launch-ready.

---

## 2. The belief

I believe most builders do not fail because they launch badly.

They fail because they build in isolation for too long.

By the time they post on LinkedIn, Product Hunt, WhatsApp groups, or Reddit, the product is already shaped. Feedback comes late. Often, it is either too polite, too shallow, or too scattered to act on.

The internet has many places to announce what you have built.  
It has very few places to improve what you are building.

That is the gap Curious Geeks is trying to solve.

---

## 3. Mission

**Help builders improve faster by giving them useful feedback before they launch.**

Curious Geeks exists for people who are building something, but do not yet have access to a strong network, advisors, users, or product thinkers who can tell them what is working and what is not.

The goal is simple:

**No builder should have to wait until launch day to learn what users actually think.**

---

## 4. Vision

**Every builder should have an advisory board before they launch.**

Today, that is only available to people with strong networks, mentors, accelerators, or investors.

Curious Geeks can make that accessible to everyone by combining:

1. A community of builders
2. Structured feedback loops
3. Reputation-based reviewers
4. AI that summarizes, clusters, and turns feedback into next actions

The long-term vision is to become the place where early products evolve through continuous feedback.

Not a launch platform.  
A product improvement platform.

---

## 5. Why now

Three shifts make this the right time:

### 1. More people are building than ever before

AI tools, no-code tools, templates, and open-source projects have made it easier for students, PMs, designers, engineers, and indie hackers to build real products quickly.

But building has become easier than validating.

### 2. Launch platforms are optimized for attention, not learning

Product Hunt is great for visibility. LinkedIn is great for distribution. Reddit is great for raw reactions.

But none of them are designed around structured product improvement.

A builder does not just need upvotes.  
They need useful answers to questions like:

- Is the problem clear?
- Is the product understandable?
- Would someone use this?
- What is confusing?
- What should I fix first?
- Is this launch-ready?

### 3. AI can convert messy feedback into clear direction

Community feedback alone can become noisy.

AI can act like a feedback analyst:

- Summarize feedback
- Detect repeated objections
- Cluster feature requests
- Identify unclear positioning
- Turn comments into next steps
- Track how the product improves over time

That is where Curious Geeks can become more than a community.

It can become an AI-powered product improvement system.

## 6. The user

Curious Geeks is for early builders.

### Primary users

1. **Indie builders**  
   People building side projects, tools, directories, SaaS products, or experiments.

2. **Students and early-career builders**  
   People who are learning product, design, coding, AI, or entrepreneurship and want feedback on real work.

3. **Product managers and designers**  
   People who want to test ideas, workflows, prototypes, landing pages, and MVPs before investing more time.

4. **Hackathon and community builders**  
   People who build fast, but need useful feedback to decide what to do next.

## 7. The problem

The current feedback journey is broken.

A builder usually does this:

1. Gets an idea
2. Builds alone
3. Shows it to friends
4. Gets polite feedback
5. Posts publicly
6. Receives scattered reactions
7. Struggles to decide what to change
8. Either abandons the product or keeps building without clarity

The core problem is not lack of feedback.

The core problem is lack of **useful, structured, and actionable feedback at the right time**.

## 8. Current MVP

The current MVP of Curious Geeks focuses on the first loop:

**Share project → receive feedback → improve**

The product currently supports:

- Sharing a project
- Explaining what was built
- Allowing others to give feedback
- Creating a simple feedback loop around the project

This is intentionally simple.

The goal of the MVP is not to prove every future feature.

The goal is to prove the core behavior:

**Will builders share unfinished work if the platform is designed around useful feedback?**

## 12. Product principles

### 1. Feedback should be specific

“Looks good” is not feedback.

Curious Geeks should push users to explain what is clear, what is confusing, and what they would change.

### 2. Builders should improve publicly

A project should not be a static post.

It should have versions, updates, and learning history.

### 3. AI should reduce noise, not replace the community

The community gives human judgment.

AI helps summarize, cluster, and convert feedback into next steps.

### 4. Reputation should be earned by usefulness

The best reviewers are not the loudest people.

They are the people whose feedback helps builders make better decisions.

### 5. The product should reward iteration

The platform should not only celebrate what gets posted.

It should celebrate what gets improved.

## 13. Roadmap

## Phase 1 — MVP: Feedback loop

**Status:** Built / in progress

Goal: Prove that builders are willing to share projects and receive feedback.

Core features:

- Project submission
- Project description
- Basic feedback/comments
- Simple project discovery
- Creator profile

Success signal:

- Builders post projects
- Other users leave feedback
- Creators respond to feedback
- Creators come back to update or share again

---

## Phase 2 — Structured feedback

**Timeline:** Next 30 days

Goal: Make feedback more useful and less random.

Features:

- Feedback templates
- Feedback categories
  - Clarity
  - Problem
  - UX
  - Positioning
  - Pricing
  - Launch readiness
- “Ask for feedback on this” prompt
- Feedback quality indicators
- Creator response flow
- Basic notification loop

Example feedback prompts:

- What is unclear about this product?
- Would you use this? Why or why not?
- What is the weakest part of the current experience?
- What should the builder improve before launch?
- What similar product does this remind you of?

Success metric:

- Average useful feedback per project
- Creator response rate
- Repeat project updates

---

## Phase 3 — Builder iteration log

**Timeline:** 60–90 days

Goal: Turn each project into a living product journey.

Features:

- Version history
- “What changed?” updates
- Before/after project updates
- Feedback status:
  - Open
  - Accepted
  - Rejected
  - Shipped
- Changelog for builders
- Follow a project
- Weekly builder updates

Why this matters:

A product should not be judged only by its first post.

The real signal is whether the builder listens, learns, and improves.

Success metric:

- Number of feedback items converted into product changes
- Number of projects with multiple updates
- Project follower retention

---

## Phase 4 — AI Feedback Analyst

**Timeline:** 3–6 months

Goal: Help builders understand the signal hidden inside feedback.

Features:

- AI feedback summary
- Feedback clustering
- Repeated objections
- Top requested changes
- Confusing areas
- Launch readiness summary
- Suggested next actions

Example output:

> “Most reviewers understand the target user, but 5 out of 8 people were unclear about the pricing. The strongest repeated request is a demo video. Recommended next step: add a 30-second demo and rewrite the hero section.”

Why this matters:

Human feedback can be messy.

AI turns it into product direction.

Success metric:

- Percentage of builders who act on AI-summarized feedback
- Time from feedback received to product update
- Improvement in launch-readiness score after iteration

---

## Phase 5 — Reviewer reputation system

**Timeline:** 6–9 months

Goal: Make high-quality feedback visible and rewarded.

Features:

- Reviewer profiles
- Feedback helpfulness votes
- Domain tags
  - SaaS
  - AI tools
  - Consumer apps
  - Design
  - Growth
  - Pricing
- Reviewer credibility score
- Top reviewers leaderboard
- Invite-only expert reviewers

Why this matters:

The platform will only work if feedback quality stays high.

Reputation makes useful feedback more visible and encourages people to give better feedback.

Success metric:

- Helpful feedback ratio
- Repeat reviewer activity
- Feedback-to-improvement conversion rate

---

## Phase 6 — AI Builder Advisor

**Timeline:** 9–12 months

Goal: Make Curious Geeks feel like an AI product advisor for every builder.

Features:

- Product health score
- Launch-readiness score
- Competitor comparison
- User persona clarity check
- Positioning review
- Landing page review
- Experiment recommendations
- Next-best-action suggestions

Example output:

This is the step where Curious Geeks becomes more than a feedback platform.

It becomes an AI companion for builders.

Goal: Give every builder the kind of advisory support usually available only to funded startups.

Long-term capabilities:

- AI remembers past feedback

- AI recommends what to build next
- AI identifies when a product is ready to launch

In the long run, Curious Geeks should answer one question:

**What should this builder do next to improve the product?**

## 19. Risks and how I would handle them

### Risk 1: Low-quality feedback

Solution:

- Structured prompts
- Feedback categories
- Helpful feedback votes
- Reviewer reputation
- AI moderation and summarization

### Risk 2: Builders may not want to share early

Solution:

- Allow private feedback links
- Allow limited visibility
- Encourage “work in progress” culture
- Show examples of builders improving through feedback

### Risk 3: Community cold start

Solution:

- Start with one niche: AI builders or student builders
- Manually onboard first 50 builders
- Personally invite reviewers
- Run weekly feedback drops

### Risk 4: Looks too similar to Product Hunt

Solution:

- Avoid launch language
- Focus on “before launch”
- Build around iteration, feedback, and product changes
- Make the core object a project journey, not a launch post

### Risk 5: AI gives generic advice

Solution:

- Ground AI in actual project context and human feedback
- Use AI to summarize and prioritize, not replace human judgment
- Let builders rate AI usefulness
