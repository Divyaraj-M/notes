# 08 · Negotiation & Objection Handling

> [!abstract] Goal
> Reach terms both sides can live with, while protecting price integrity and the future relationship.

| **Owner** | AE + Sales Manager + Deal Desk |

## Process — step by step

### 1. Anticipate the objections
Price, timing, incumbent contract, internal capacity, security, and "we can build this". Have a documented response and proof point for each.

- **Metrics to measure:** objection frequency by type · % of objections with a library response · objection → resolution rate

### 2. Diagnose before answering
Every objection is either a misunderstanding, a missing proof point, a real constraint, or a stalling tactic. Ask a question before you respond.

- **Metrics to measure:** median time to resolve an objection · reopened-objection count per deal · % of objections misclassified (found in loss reviews)

### 3. Never discount for free
Trade every concession: longer term, upfront payment, more seats, a case study, a reference call, a faster signature date.

- **Metrics to measure:** concessions given vs received ratio · average discount % · % of deals closed at zero discount · value of non-price concessions captured

### 4. Hold the value frame
Return to the cost-of-inaction number from [[Discovery]] whenever the conversation drifts to price alone.

- **Metrics to measure:** average discount on deals with a quantified ROI model vs without · price realisation % (net ÷ list)

### 5. Know your walk-away
Floor price, minimum term, non-negotiable clauses — agreed with your manager **before** the call, not during it.

- **Metrics to measure:** % of deals closed below floor price · escalation/approval count per deal · walked-away deal count

### 6. Handle the competitor honestly
Differentiate on the criteria they ranked highest. Never disparage; reframe.

- **Metrics to measure:** win rate vs each named competitor · competitive loss reason distribution · displacement rate against the incumbent

### 7. Manage the stall
"Let's revisit next quarter" is usually a missing stakeholder or an unquantified case. Find which one — don't just follow up.

- **Metrics to measure:** push/slip rate · number of pushed close dates per deal · stalled-deal recovery rate · % lost to "no decision"

### 8. Lock the final terms in writing
Once agreed, restate everything in an email before it goes to paper. Verbal agreements mutate between calls.

- **Metrics to measure:** % with written term confirmation before paper · post-signature term disputes · order-form rework caused by unclear terms

## Stage scorecard

| Metric | What it tells you | How to calculate | Directional benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Average discount % | Whether value selling is working | 1 − (net ÷ list) | <15% |
| Win rate | Overall late-stage effectiveness | Won ÷ (won + lost) | 20–30% from SQL |
| Slip rate | Forecast discipline | Deals with a pushed close date ÷ total | <25% |
| Price realisation | Pricing power | Net ACV ÷ list ACV | >85% |
| Concession ratio | Negotiation discipline | Concessions received ÷ given | ≥1.0 |

## What you need going in
- Approved discount authority and floor price
- Objection-handling library
- Competitive battlecards
- Reference customers on standby

## What this stage produces
- Agreed commercial terms
- Final quote
- Documented concessions and what was traded for each

> [!success] Exit criteria
> Commercial terms are agreed **in writing** and the contract can be sent for review.

## Where deals die here
- Discounting at the first sign of resistance
- Negotiating with someone who can't sign
- Conceding before the objection is even raised
- Leaving terms verbal
