# 01 · ICP & Territory Planning

> [!abstract] Goal
> Decide who is worth selling to before spending a single hour selling. Everything downstream — conversion rate, cycle length, churn — is set here.

|  |  |
|---|---|
| **Phase** | Before a deal exists |
| **Owner** | Sales leadership + Marketing + RevOps |
| **Typical duration** | Quarterly / annual exercise |

## Process — step by step

### 1. Analyse the existing base
Pull closed-won and churned accounts. Look at which industries, company sizes, use cases and buyer titles **retained and expanded** — not just which ones bought.

- **Metrics to measure:** win rate by segment · net revenue retention (NRR) by segment · logo churn by segment · average ACV by segment · CAC payback by segment

### 2. Define the ICP
Firmographics (industry, headcount, geo, tech stack), the trigger event, and the job-to-be-done. Write down disqualifiers as explicitly as qualifiers.

- **Metrics to measure:** % of closed-won matching ICP · win rate ICP vs non-ICP · ICP-fit score coverage across the account base

### 3. Size and segment the market
Build the total addressable list, then split into tiers — Tier 1 named accounts, Tier 2 volume, Tier 3 inbound-only.

- **Metrics to measure:** TAM / SAM / SOM · addressable accounts per tier · % of TAM already touched · white-space accounts remaining

### 4. Assign territories and quotas
Split by geo, segment or vertical. Set quota, coverage ratio and the activity model that gets you there.

- **Metrics to measure:** pipeline coverage ratio (target 3–4× quota) · accounts per rep · quota capacity vs plan · territory balance (variance in account value per rep)

### 5. Agree the pipeline definitions
Lock stage names, entry/exit criteria and what an MQL, SQL and Opportunity actually mean. Configure them in the CRM so the funnel is measurable.

- **Metrics to measure:** stage-to-stage conversion rates (are they computable at all?) · CRM required-field completeness % · % of opps with a valid stage and next step

### 6. Build the account plan for Tier 1
Org chart, incumbent vendor, renewal date of that incumbent, known initiatives, relationship map.

- **Metrics to measure:** % of Tier 1 accounts with a completed plan · mapped contacts per Tier 1 account · % with incumbent renewal date captured

## Stage scorecard

| Metric | What it tells you | How to calculate | Directional benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Win rate by segment | Which segments you actually beat the market in | Won ÷ (won + lost) per segment | Compare relative, not absolute |
| NRR by segment | Whether the segment is worth acquiring at all | (Start ARR + expansion − churn − contraction) ÷ start ARR | >100% healthy; >110% strong |
| Pipeline coverage ratio | Whether the territory can mathematically hit quota | Open pipeline ÷ quota | 3–4× for mid-market |
| % closed-won matching ICP | Whether the ICP is real or aspirational | ICP-matching wins ÷ total wins | >70% |
| TAM / SAM / SOM | Whether the plan has headroom | Bottom-up account count × ACV | — |

> [!warning] Treat benchmarks as starting rules of thumb, not truth. Replace each one with your own trailing-four-quarter baseline as soon as you have it.

## What you need going in
- Closed-won and churn data
- Product usage and retention data
- Market research, analyst reports
- Last year's quota attainment

## What this stage produces
- Written ICP doc with explicit disqualifiers
- Tiered target account list
- Territory and quota assignments
- CRM stage definitions

> [!success] Exit criteria
> You can name, in one sentence, who you sell to **and who you refuse to sell to** — and the target list exists in the CRM.

## Where deals die here
- Defining ICP from who bought, ignoring who stayed
- Territories with no account list behind them
- Stage definitions that live in a doc and not in the CRM
