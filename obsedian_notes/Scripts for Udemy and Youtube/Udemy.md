# Module 1: PM Reality — From Authority Myths to Execution Truth

## Module spine (one-line logic)

PMs don’t win with authority or ideas.  
They win by **reducing confusion through decisions, execution, and documentation**.

Everything below ladders to that.

---

## Lesson 1: [[PM is not the owner or CEO of the product]]

**Why this comes first**  
If this belief is wrong, everything else breaks.

**Core ideas**

- PM does not own authority. PM owns clarity.
- PM is not a mini-CEO.
- Influence comes from reasoning, not control.
- Decisions must be backed by research and data.
- There is no fixed winning strategy. Product is trial → feedback → correction.

**Link to next lesson**  
Because PMs don’t own authority, they must rely on **how they make decisions**, not titles.

➡️ _So the real PM output is decisions, not features._

---

## Lesson 2: [[Decisions are the real output of PMs]]

**Why this follows Lesson 1**  
If you don’t own authority, decisions are how you create impact.

**Core ideas**

- Features are side effects. Decisions are the job.
    
- PMs are judged on decision quality + decision speed.
    
- Not all decisions deserve the same rigor.
    
- Speed matters, but blind speed causes damage.
    
- AI helps with speed, not judgment.
    

Introduce the key tension:

- Speed vs precision
    
- Learning vs risk
    

**Link to next lesson**  
To decide well, PMs need a way to **simplify reality**.

➡️ _That simplification is called a mental model._

---

## Lesson 3: [[Mental models — how PMs actually think]]

**Why this comes here**  
You can’t make good decisions without a mental model.

**Core ideas**

- Mental model = how you simplify complexity to decide.
    
- Every PM has one, knowingly or not.
    
- Product mental model explains:
    
    - Who the user is
        
    - Where value is created
        
    - Where friction kills usage
        
- Most features are not invented.
    
- Don’t chase innovation. Observe, copy, adapt to context.
    
- Fit > novelty.
    

**Link to next lesson**  
Mental models guide _what_ to do.  
But early PMs don’t get to redefine the game yet.

➡️ _They must execute within constraints._

---

## Lesson 4: [[The real job of a starter PM — execution]]

**Why this follows mental models**  
Even with good thinking, beginners don’t own strategy.

**Core ideas**

- Strategy and roadmaps are usually set by management.
    
- Early PMs are hired to execute, not redefine vision.
    
- Real PM work is:
    
    - Working with design
        
    - Working with devs
        
    - Aligning stakeholders
        
    - Translating intent into shippable work
        
- Domain knowledge is non-negotiable.
        
    - Software → Git, GitHub, staging, prod
        
- Without domain knowledge, PMs lose trust fast.
    

**Link to next lesson**  
Execution creates decisions, changes, and assumptions every day.

If these are not captured, confusion returns.

➡️ _That’s why documentation becomes the PM superpower._

---

## Lesson 5: [[Documentation is the real PM superpower]]

**Why this is the final lesson**  
Documentation ties **everything** together.

**Core ideas**

- PM work disappears if it’s not written.
    
- You must document:
    
    - PRDs (at the right depth)
        
    - Decisions and trade-offs
        
    - Stakeholder discussions
        
    - Assumptions and changes
        
- PRDs don’t stand alone. They link to context.
    
- PMs must understand the product as a system:
    
    - If I touch this, what breaks?
        
- Tools don’t matter. Clear thinking does.
    
- Documentation creates speed, trust, and memory.
    

**Final closure**  
Documentation preserves:

- Your decisions
    
- Your reasoning
    
- Your credibility
