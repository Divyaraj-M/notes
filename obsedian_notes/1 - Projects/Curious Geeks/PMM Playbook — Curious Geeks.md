> **Operating model (read this first):**
> 
> - **PMM OKR** = grow WAEM
> - **Primary channel** = LinkedIn (awareness)
> - **Supply engine** = direct maker outreach (product submissions)
> - **Retention engine** = content engine (blog + newsletter + weekly recap)
> - **Quality guardrail** = every product gets a thoughtful review within 24 hours
> 
> Don't build a big distribution machine. Build a small feedback loop that feels alive.

**OKR tells us what to move. Channels tell us how we move it.** Distribution is not a separate plan — it's the engine _inside_ the objective.

---

## The North Star

**WAEM = Weekly Active Engaged Members** — members who liked, reviewed, or commented in the last 7 days. If WAEM is flat, the room is dead, no matter how good the traffic looks.

---

## Part 1 — The objective

**Objective: Make Curious Geeks feel alive.**

Key Results:

1. WAEM reaches **15%** of total members
2. **Every product gets ≥1 thoughtful review within 24 hours**
3. **10 product submissions** in 30 days
4. **4 teardown/content posts** in 30 days
5. Email list to **25 subscribers** (collected quietly; see Channel 3)

Distribution without engagement is empty traffic — that's why the objective is "feel alive," not "get signups."

---

## Part 1b — Teardown & engagement (phased rollout)

The teardown model changes as the community grows. Don't start with contests — start with you doing the work, then hand it to the community.

### Phase 1 — Now (small): full teardown for every product

While volume is low, give **every** submitted product a full teardown. It's doable, it builds trust fast, and it earns word-of-mouth. This is the founder/PMM doing the work by hand — and that's correct at this stage.

### Phase 2 — Growth (can't teardown everything): shift to the community

Once submissions outpace what you can teardown, flip engagement to the members:

1. **Teardown contest** — members write teardowns of a featured product; best one wins / gets featured. Turns teardown labor into community content (UGC) and engagement.
2. **Likes** — already live; make them count toward rankings.
3. **Best-liked product — week & month** — recognition that drives makers to submit and members to vote.
4. **Trending section** — surfaces what's hot right now; gives returning visitors a reason to come back.

> [!note] **Why this matters for WAEM:** Phase 2 mechanics (contests, likes, rankings, trending) are engagement engines — they manufacture reviews, comments, and return visits _without you writing every teardown_. That's how WAEM scales past founder effort.

**Rule of thumb for the switch:** stay in Phase 1 until you literally can't keep up with full teardowns within the week. That backlog is the signal to introduce contests + rankings.

## Part 2 — The three channels

Use **only 3** active channels right now. Not 5, not 10.

### Channel 1 — LinkedIn _(awareness)_

Your main public channel. Attract PMs, SaaS people, founders, builders. Post: product teardowns · build-in-public updates · featured products · open calls for makers.

### Channel 2 — Direct maker outreach _(product submissions)_

Not optional. Early Curious Geeks will not grow by posting alone — you need supply. DM builders: _"I saw you're building X. I'm building Curious Geeks, a space where builders get thoughtful product feedback. Want me to feature your product this week?"_ **Target: ~20 makers/week.**

### Channel 3 — Content Engine _(retention)_

One core post, repurposed three ways — no separate work:

1. **Blog post** — lives on the site, good for SEO/long-term discovery.
2. **Newsletter** — same content emailed to subscribers. Send it even if only 5 people get it.
3. **LinkedIn recap** — short version that pulls people back to the site.

> [!note] **No email list yet? You still publish.** A newsletter is a _content asset_ first, an email second. Run the weekly recap as a blog + LinkedIn post now, and add one simple capture box on the site ("Get weekly product teardowns and builder discoveries → Join Curious Geeks Weekly"). Once you cross ~25 emails, send the same recap by email too. Don't overbuild it.

**Weekly content format — "Curious Geeks Weekly":** Featured product of the week · 2 more interesting products · 1 product teardown · 1 builder lesson · CTA (submit / review).

### Channel priority (everything else)

|Channel|Status|Why|
|---|---|---|
|LinkedIn|**Primary**|Best fit for the PM/founder audience|
|Direct DMs|**Primary**|Needed for product supply|
|Content engine|**Primary**|Blog + newsletter + recap = retention|
|SEO / AEO|Secondary|Good long-term, but slow|
|Reddit|Later|High effort, easy to look spammy|
|Discord|Not now|An empty community feels dead|
|Indie Hackers|Later|Useful once you have proof/content|

## Part 3 — Daily routine (60–90 min)

1. **Check dashboard** (5 min) — WAEM, new members, submissions, reviews/comments, return visits. Ask one question: _which input is weak today?_
2. **Keep the room alive** (20 min) — every new product gets a response. Early on, write the first review yourself. A product with zero feedback kills trust.
3. **Recruit makers** (20 min) — DM 2–3 builders (LinkedIn, Product Hunt, Indie Hackers, X, college makers, SaaS founders). Goal: 10 submissions / 30 days.
4. **Distribute on LinkedIn** (20 min) — post one: teardown insight · featured product · build-in-public · "what I learned reviewing X" · open call.
5. **Log one learning** (5 min) — e.g. "Teardown post got 3 comments, open call got 0 — teardown angle works better." This becomes your growth intelligence.

> [!tip] **When time is short:** keep the room alive > recruit makers > distribute. A dead room can't be marketed.

## Part 5 — The metric map

|Type|Metric|Why it matters|
|---|---|---|
|**North Star**|WAEM|Shows if the room is alive|
|Input KPI|Product submissions|Supply-side growth|
|Input KPI|Reviews/comments per product|Engagement quality|
|Input KPI|Weekly signups|Audience growth|
|Input KPI|Email clicks / return visits|Retention|
|**KRI**|**Products with zero review after 24h**|**The #1 danger signal — room feels dead**|
|KRI|Low-quality reviews|Feedback culture decaying|
|KRI|Spam signups|Community quality dropping|
|KRI|Site errors|Visitors bounce permanently|

### What OKR / KPI / KRI mean (plain English)

- **OKR** — the ambitious goal you push _this period_ (the objective above + its KRs). Aim ~70% achievable.
- **KPI** — a metric you watch _continuously_ (WAEM, submissions, signups). WAEM is the headline KPI.
- **KRI** — an _early-warning_ metric with a threshold. It moves before a KPI tanks, so you can act early. Most important KRI right now: **any product with zero thoughtful review after 24h.**
