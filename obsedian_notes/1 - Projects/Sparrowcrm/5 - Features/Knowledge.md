## 1. Why this document exists

"Knowledge" is easy to wave at and hard to bound — left undefined, it becomes a dumping ground for anything vaguely informative, and the intelligence layer ends up reasoning over a pile no one governs. This document defines what Knowledge _is_, why it is a peer to the intelligence layer rather than a part of it, the three layers it is built from, and the first principle that separates a living knowledge system from a stale file dump. Layer-level detail lives in the three companion docs.

---

## 2. Definition

> **Knowledge is what the CRM Intelligence layer reasons _with_ — as opposed to what it reasons _about_.**

Two terms carry this whole model, so they're worth defining plainly:

- **Reasons _about_** = the **subject** of an action — the thing being examined, judged, or changed. When an agent checks a deal's stage, the deal is what it reasons _about_. This is Layer 1, records.
- **Reasons _with_** = the **backdrop brought to** that action — what the layer already knows before it looks at the subject. When the agent judges that stage, the workspace's stage rules and past win/loss patterns are what it reasons _with_. This is Layers 2 and 3.

The same fact can be either, depending on its role in the moment. "Acme lost to Salesforce" is reasoned _about_ when Acme is the deal in front of you; the _pattern_ "we lose to Salesforce on price" distilled from Acme and forty others is reasoned _with_ on the next deal. Subject vs. backdrop is the distinction — not the content.

- The CRM's **records** are what intelligence reasons _about_ — the subject of an action. A deal is a thing an agent acts on.
- **Knowledge** is everything the layer brings _to_ that reasoning — the instance in front of it, the workspace's working memory, and the patterns learned across everything that came before.

CRM Intelligence is the **engine** (Agents, Signals, Zulie); Knowledge is the **fuel** they draw on. It produces no suggestions or answers of its own — it makes everything the layer produces sharper, more workspace-specific, and more informed by what the company has already learned.

## 3. The three layers

Knowledge is built from three layers, distinguished by **altitude** — how far each sits above a single instance — and by **how it enters the system.**

| Layer                             | What it is                                                                                           | Altitude                            | How it enters                           | Reasoned _about_ or _with_           |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Layer 1 — [[Records-L1]] **        | All CRM data: contacts, companies, deals, lists, tasks, notes, emails, meeting transcripts, activity | Instance (one entity)               | Generated by usage                      | Reasoned _about_                     |
| **Layer 2 — [[Context-L2]] **        | Structured working memory: ICP, business profile, process conventions, terminology                   | Workspace (the business as a whole) | Deliberately authored by admins         | Reasoned _with_, always injected     |
| **Layer 3 — [[Knowledge Base-L3]] ** | Patterns distilled across records + authored material (battlecards, process docs)                    | Pattern (across many instances)     | Distilled by agents/humans, or uploaded | Reasoned _with_, retrieved on demand |

_Sequencing note: Layer 1 is already present; Layer 3 (Knowledge Base) ships first; Layer 2 (Context) is v2. Until L2 exists, capabilities that depend on it — deal-hygiene stage checks, ICP-dependent signals, Zulie fit-answers — are deferred or run on defaults._

The three are not redundant — they sit at different altitudes:

- A **record** says what happened to _one_ entity ("Acme lost to Salesforce on price").
- **Context** says how _this workspace_ operates ("our Negotiation stage requires a sent proposal").
- **Knowledge Base** says what is true _across many_ records ("we lose healthcare deals under 200 employees to Salesforce on price 60% of the time; the ones we win lead with implementation speed").

## 4. First Principle

> **Knowledge is pattern, not instance. Each layer earns its place by holding what the layer below it cannot. Layer 3 exists to hold what is true _across_ records — the lessons, patterns, and rationale no single record contains — and to keep that knowledge alive as new records confirm or contradict it. Anything that merely restates a fact already on a record is not knowledge; it is duplication.**

This resolves the apparent conflict between layers. "Acme lost to Salesforce on price" is **not** knowledge to be copied into Layer 3 — it is a Layer 1 fact, and it stays there. The _pattern across fifty Acme-like deals_ is knowledge, and it lives nowhere in the CRM today because no record is about it. The KB earns its existence only by rising above the instance.

**The test — the anti-dump law:** _Is this just a fact already on a record, or a lesson that lives across records?_ If it is already on a record, it belongs on the record. If it rises above any single record — and is owned, dated, and verifiable — it belongs in Layer 3. A KB full of things you could have found by reading one record is a dump; a KB full of things you _couldn't_ is a knowledge system.

## 5. The boundary — what is and isn't Knowledge

Knowledge (Layers 2 and 3) is **deliberately established grounding**, not **captured operational data**. The test: _did a human or agent deliberately establish this as reusable grounding, or did the system capture it as a byproduct of work?_

- Established → Knowledge: ICP definition, stage rules (L2); win/loss patterns, battlecards, process docs (L3).
- Captured → not Knowledge, it's Layer 1 records: call transcripts, email activity, computed signals, record history.

Transcripts and signals _inform_ the layer, but they are the instance data reasoned _about_ — the raw material from which L3 patterns are later distilled, not knowledge themselves.

## 6. Grounding is selective, not ambient

A division reaches for Knowledge only when the job needs it, and _which_ layer it reaches for depends on the job. Nothing is force-fed grounding it doesn't use. Rule of thumb: the more outward-facing and judgment-heavy a capability, the more Knowledge it needs; pure record-vs-record work needs little or none.

- **Agents — per-agent, declared in each spec.** Data hygiene reasons about records against activity evidence and needs _no_ Layer 2/3 (a stale title is fact-vs-fact); deal hygiene touches only Layer 2 process conventions, only for the stage-consistency check. Research/prospecting lean on Layer 2 ICP and eventually Layer 3 patterns.
- **Signals — only ICP-dependent ones.** Behavioral signals (best-time-to-contact) touch no Knowledge. Buying intent / buyer profile score against **structured** Layer 2 ICP attributes — which is why ICP must be fields and ranges, not prose.
- **Zulie — by a defined retrieval policy.** Record question → Layer 1 only; fit/strategy question → Layer 2 Context; "how do we handle X" → Layer 3 KB. Which question type reaches which layer is a routing policy, designed deliberately.

## 7. The trust dividend

Because Knowledge is deliberately established, a capability that cites it is defensible in a way that imposing a generic opinion never is. "Your definition says Negotiation requires a sent proposal" (L2) or "across your last ten healthcare losses, price was the blocker" (L3) is the workspace's own knowledge reflected back — not the AI's opinion. This is what makes grounded suggestions acceptable rather than presumptuous.
